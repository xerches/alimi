# Closing-Day Checklist for Massachusetts Home Buyers and Sellers

Provided by **{{Firm}}**. Questions or anything that feels wrong: call us on **{{Firm Phone}}**, the number from your engagement letter, not a number from an email.

## Why you are reading this

Buying or selling a home is the largest payment most people ever make, and criminals know the date. They break into or imitate the email of someone in the deal (an agent, a lender, a law office, sometimes the buyer) and send wire instructions that look exactly right, days or hours before closing. The money goes to the criminal's account and is moved on within hours. In the FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report, published in April 2026, real estate fraud alone accounted for 12,368 complaints and $275.1 million in reported losses in one year.

In Massachusetts, a closing is conducted by an attorney. The buyer's funds go to that attorney's client trust account (an **IOLTA** account, short for Interest on Lawyers' Trust Accounts) by wire or bank check. State law (the Good Funds statute, M.G.L. c. 183 §63B) means the closing attorney must be holding cleared funds before the deed and mortgage are recorded, so large sums move in the day or two before closing. That window is what the criminals target.

One rule protects you from almost all of it: **before you send money, call the closing attorney's office on a number you got somewhere other than the email, and have them read the account details to you.**

## For buyers

### When you hire the closing attorney

- [ ] Write the firm's main phone number here, taken from the engagement letter or the firm's website that you typed into your browser: ______________________. You can confirm an attorney's name and office through the Massachusetts Board of Bar Overseers lookup at massbbo.org.
- [ ] Ask the firm how they deliver wire instructions. The answer should be "by phone, in person or through our secure portal", and "they will not change". Write down the name of the person who will handle your funds: ______________________.
- [ ] Ask whether you may bring a bank check (a treasurer's or cashier's check made out to the firm's trust account) instead of wiring. Many Massachusetts closing attorneys accept one for the buyer's funds. It cannot be redirected by an email.
- [ ] Tell your real estate agent and your lender that you will only accept wire instructions from the closing attorney, confirmed by phone. Agents and lenders should never send them.

### When wire instructions arrive

- [ ] Do not act on instructions that arrive by email, text or a messaging app on their own, even from an address you recognise, and even if they look like a PDF on letterhead.
- [ ] Call the firm on the number you wrote down above. Ask for the person you named. Have them read you the bank name, the account name (it should be the firm's trust or IOLTA account, never a person's name) and the account and routing numbers. Compare digit by digit with what you received. Do not read the numbers out yourself.
- [ ] If anything differs, or if you receive "updated" instructions at any point, stop and call the firm. Real instructions do not change. A change notice is the fraud.
- [ ] Never email your bank details, Social Security number or a photo of your ID to anyone in the transaction. Use the firm's secure portal or deliver them in person.

### Sending the money

- [ ] Wire from a branch or over the phone with your bank if you can, and ask the bank to confirm the account name on the receiving account matches the firm.
- [ ] Send the day before closing, not the morning of, so there is time to fix a problem.
- [ ] Within an hour of sending, call the firm on your written-down number and confirm they received it. Do not rely on an email confirmation.
- [ ] If you must bring a bank check instead, carry it yourself and get a receipt.

### On closing day

- [ ] Bring government ID and the cashier's check if you are using one.
- [ ] Ignore any last-minute message about a "shortfall", a "new account" or a "corrected" amount. Call the firm.
- [ ] After closing, be suspicious of any email offering a refund or asking for a further payment. Call before you do anything.

## For sellers

- [ ] Give the closing attorney your instructions for receiving the proceeds **in person at the closing, with identification**, or by phone on a call the firm places to the number in their file. Never by email.
- [ ] If anyone emails you asking for your bank details "so the proceeds can be sent", call the firm. They will not ask by email.
- [ ] Expect the firm to verify your mortgage payoff figure directly with your lender, by phone on a number from the lender's statement. If you are asked to supply the payoff instructions yourself, call the firm.
- [ ] Ask whether you can receive proceeds by bank check at the table if you prefer not to share account details at all.
- [ ] Once the proceeds arrive, do not send money back to anyone claiming an "overpayment". Call the firm.

## If something is wrong

Minutes matter. Do these in order.

1. **Call your bank's wire department** and tell them the transfer was fraudulent. Ask them to recall it and to contact the receiving bank. Have the amount, date, and account numbers ready.
2. **Call the closing attorney** on your written-down number so they can alert their bank and the other parties.
3. **Report to the FBI** at ic3.gov the same day, and call the FBI Boston field office on 857-386-2000. For wires of $50,000 or more sent internationally, mention that you are asking for the Financial Fraud Kill Chain within 72 hours.
4. Keep every email and text, including headers, and do not delete anything from the account that received them.
5. Tell your agent and your lender, so that nobody else in the transaction sends money.

## For the closing office

This page is for {{Firm}}'s own staff and is also the standard clients should expect from any Massachusetts closing attorney.

- [ ] Wire instructions are delivered by phone, in person or through the secure portal at engagement, with a cover note saying they will never change. They are never sent by plain email, and the engagement letter and every email signature say so.
- [ ] Every client receives this checklist and an acknowledgement to sign, modelled on the Massachusetts Association of Realtors' wire fraud advisory (Form #519), which tells buyers and sellers to "never wire funds without personally speaking with the intended recipient" and to call "using a phone number that has been independently obtained, not the phone number contained in the email".
- [ ] Payoff statements are verified by calling the lender on a number from the lender's own statement or website, never from the payoff letter's email, before any payoff wire. Changed payoff instructions are treated as fraud until proven otherwise.
- [ ] Seller proceeds instructions are taken in person with identification, or on a call the firm initiates, and are logged.
- [ ] Incoming funds are matched to the client by name and amount, and the client is called to confirm receipt.
- [ ] Staff email uses multi-factor authentication, external forwarding is blocked, and mailbox rules that forward or delete are reviewed, so that the firm's own email is not the weak point. The Payment Verification Procedure in this kit covers the firm's own payments.
- [ ] The firm's professional liability and cyber policies are checked for social engineering and funds transfer fraud coverage, and the insurer's reporting deadline is written in the incident plan.
- [ ] If a client's funds are diverted: bank recall request, FBI Boston 857-386-2000 and ic3.gov, the title insurer's claims line, the firm's insurer, and a frank call to the client, all within the first hours. Keep the headers of the fraudulent email.
- [ ] Once a year, staff run through a drill: a realistic fake payoff letter or buyer wire request, and a review of what was caught.
