# Three areas to build first

Oct 31, 2026 · Alex

Source: Claude Docs, https://claude.ai/artifact/5Xsmabw1PnuLHPtfSamXFE. Written before the rename, so "Caprius" means Alimi. The build order changed on Nov 1, 2026 (scanner first, then the fraud kit, then the platform); see CLAUDE.md.

## Bottom line

Build three things for one buyer: the owner or operations lead of a 10 to 200 person company that cannot hire security staff. The three are an **AI usage policy and inventory kit**, a **cloud workspace security scanner that produces an insurer-ready report**, and a **payment fraud protection kit** for finance teams and real estate closings.

They fit together because the same referral partners (insurance brokers, CPAs, business and real estate attorneys) send all three, each one has a free or cheap product version that generates leads and a paid service version that generates income, and none of them needs an entity, insurance or on-call coverage to start building today.

Nothing here depends on the Caprius name. Build the products under a working name, validate with 15 partner conversations and 3 free pilots, and form the LLC when the first paid engagement is ready to sign. That is roughly 90 days of evenings and weekends.

## What you can start now without an LLC

Almost everything except taking money under a brand name. In Massachusetts you are a sole proprietor by default, so building, publishing, talking to prospects and running free pilots are all fine today.

| Do now | Wait for the LLC and insurance |
| --- | --- |
| Build all three products against your own test tenants and mock data | Paid engagements under the brand name |
| Publish a landing page per product with a waitlist form | Signing an MSA, SOW or NDA as the company |
| Write 2 articles per area (the research doc has the hooks) and post on LinkedIn | Touching a real client's tenant, even for free, without a signed one-page authorization |
| Hold 15 conversations with 5 brokers, 5 attorneys and 5 CPAs to test the pitch | Reselling licenses or software |
| Run 3 free pilots of the scanner for companies you know, under a written permission letter | Hiring or paying contractors under the brand |
| Pick the name, check it, buy the domain and register social handles | Trademark filing (do it once the name has survived 90 days) |
| Open a separate personal bank account to keep business spending clean | Business bank account (needs the EIN and LLC papers) |
| Draft the MSA, SOW and authorization letter so they are ready to sign | Cyber liability and E&O insurance |

Three cautions. Read Rhythm's outside-work and IP clauses before you publish anything under a brand, because some agreements claim side projects built while employed. Keep every pilot on tenants you own or have written permission for. And if a friend insists on paying before the LLC exists, invoice as yourself, report it as sole-proprietor income, and keep it small; the risk is liability, not legality.

## 90-day build plan

The plan assumes 8 to 10 hours a week and your CISSP exam on November 30, so the first two weeks are light.

1. **Weeks 1-2 (to Nov 14): decide and set up.** Confirm the three areas, pick a working name, buy the domain, create one Microsoft 365 Business Premium tenant and one Google Workspace tenant for testing, and start a repo with a CLAUDE.md that describes the three products. Draft the payment fraud procedure document in Claude since it needs no code.
2. **Weeks 3-4 (to Nov 28): ship the fraud kit.** Finish the kit (procedure, scripts, poster, checklist), record the training video, put up a landing page with a waitlist. Sit the CISSP on Nov 30.
3. **Weeks 5-8 (Dec): build the scanner.** Microsoft Graph read-only checks first (MFA, legacy auth, admins, forwarding, sharing), then the Google Admin SDK module, then the insurer-ready HTML report. Run it against your two test tenants until the report reads like something a broker would accept.
4. **Weeks 9-10 (early Jan): build the AI policy generator.** Questionnaire to policy to one-pager, plus the AI app inventory pulled from the same audit logs the scanner reads. Landing page and free tier live.
5. **Weeks 11-13 (late Jan): validate.** 15 partner conversations, 3 free scanner pilots with permission letters, and a case study from each pilot. Adjust the products based on what brokers and attorneys actually ask for.
6. **Gate to form the LLC:** one partner has agreed to refer, one pilot wants the paid service, and the name has survived the checks. Then file, get insurance, open the bank account and sign the first SOW.

If the scanner slips, ship the fraud kit and the policy generator on time anyway. They prove demand with less code.

## Rebrand

Pick a name that works for a product company, not only a consultancy, because two of the three areas are software. Short, two syllables if possible, no "cyber" or "secure" in it (those words are taken a thousand times over), and nothing that ties you to Boston or to one service.

Checks before you commit, in order: the [Massachusetts business entity search](https://corp.sec.state.ma.us/corpweb/CorpSearch/CorpSearch.aspx), the [USPTO trademark search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search), the .com domain, and the LinkedIn, X and GitHub handles. A name that fails any one of these is not worth the fight.

Candidates to run through those checks, none verified yet:

- **Keelsafe** (a keel keeps a boat upright; fits "steady under pressure")
- **Northlane** (neutral, product-friendly)
- **Quietharbor** (calm, Boston without saying it)
- **Lantern Risk** (shows what is hidden; works for scans and policies)
- **Ironcrest** (strong, generic enough for software)

One alternative is to keep Caprius for the holding company and give each product its own name. That adds brand work you do not need yet. One name for everything until there is revenue.

## Startup costs, approximate

About $100 a month gets you through the 90 days. The entity and insurance add roughly $2,000 to $4,000 when you are ready to take paid work. These are approximate figures from memory, not quotes; confirm before budgeting.

| Item | Approximate cost | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Domain and hosting (Netlify or Cloudflare Pages, free tier) | $15 a year | Now |
| Microsoft 365 Business Premium test tenant, 1 user | $22 a month | Now |
| Google Workspace test tenant, 1 user | $7 to $9 a month | Now |
| Form, email and waitlist tools | $0 to $30 a month | Now |
| Massachusetts LLC filing | $500, plus $500 annual report each year | At the gate |
| Cyber liability and E&O insurance, solo consultant | $1,000 to $3,000 a year | At the gate |

Massachusetts is one of the more expensive states for an LLC, which is one more reason to wait until a paid engagement is ready. A business certificate (DBA) from your city clerk is a cheaper interim step if you want to invoice under the brand before the LLC, but it gives no liability protection.
