## The three paths, candidly

Consulting wins on fit, startup cost and time to first dollar. Software and managed services each lose on the one thing you said you want most: control over your time.

|  | Productized consulting | Managed service (MSP/MSSP) | Software product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fit with your resume | Very high, it is your current job | Medium: MSP security yes, helpdesk and 24/7 SOC no | Low-medium: you code, but have not shipped a product |
| Startup cost | Low: LLC, insurance, site, maybe $3-6K | High: tool stack $700-1,200/mo before clients, plus insurance and contracts | Moderate cash, very high time |
| Time to first revenue | Weeks, one signed SOW | Months: each client needs onboarding and a full stack | 6-18 months, often longer |
| Margin | 70-90% on your time | Industry average gross margin about 46% | High once it sells, zero until then |
| Control of your time | High: you set scope and hours | Low: outages and incidents set your schedule | High, but sales and support grow |
| Burnout risk | Medium, mostly from overbooking | High solo: you are the helpdesk and the on-call | Medium-high, long stretch without income |
| Competition | Fragmented; your SOC 2 + MSP + AI governance mix is uncommon | Boston has many MSPs at $75-200 per user | Vanta and Drata already serve 16,000+ customers |
| Path to 1-2 hires | Clear: a junior GRC analyst billed out | Needs a technician early just to cover tickets | Needs engineers before revenue |

**Why not MSP first.** Clients expect someone to answer when email is down at 7am. As a solo founder you become the helpdesk, and helpdesk volume grows with every seat you sign. That is the opposite of having a say in your own work.

**Why not software first.** The obvious products in your space (compliance automation, questionnaire answering) are owned by well-funded companies. Software built before you have paying clients usually solves the wrong problem.

**Where software does fit.** Build small internal tools that make your consulting faster: the questionnaire answer library, the M365 and Google Workspace assessment scanner, the report generator. If clients ask to buy one of them on its own, that is your product signal.

## Income math

To bring in about $16K a month, consulting needs 4 or 5 clients; an MSP needs about 210 managed users. I used $16K a month (about $190K a year before taxes and costs) as an illustration, so swap in your own target.

| Path | What it takes to reach about $16K/month | Your time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consulting | 3 retainers at $4K/mo plus one $4-6K project a month (for example a WISP or insurance readiness) | About 80-90 billable hours a month, roughly 20 a week |
| MSP (Secure tier, $165/user) | At about 46% gross margin, each user brings about $76/mo, so about 210 users, or 10-14 clients of 15-20 people | Full-time helpdesk, onboarding and on-call, more than one person can cover |

The prices come from the research in your earlier chats: retainers of $3-6K a month, fixed-fee packages of $2.5-12K, and Boston MSP tiers of $75-225 per user. Plan to keep roughly 25-35% for self-employment tax, insurance, tools and slow months.

The first income milestone can be much smaller. One $3-5K project done on nights and weekends while you are still employed proves the offer and covers your setup costs.

## Staged plan and first hires

Each stage starts only when the previous one hits its gate, so you never quit your job on hope.

1. **Side consultancy (now to about 6 months).** Pass CISSP, form the LLC, get E&O and cyber insurance, and sign 1-3 fixed-fee projects. Gate: two paid projects done and one client asking for ongoing help.
2. **Retainers while employed (about 6-12 months).** Convert project clients into $3-6K monthly retainers and join the Vanta and Drata partner programs. Gate: recurring revenue covers at least 60% of your living costs for 3 months in a row.
3. **Go full time.** Leave your job with 6 months of expenses saved. Gate: you are turning work down or booked over 80% for 2 months.
4. **First hire.** Bring on a junior GRC analyst, first as a contractor (Boston contract GRC rates run about $50-80/hr), billed to clients at $100-150/hr. They take evidence collection and questionnaires; you keep scoping, sales and audits.
5. **Second hire or managed services.** If clients keep asking you to run their security day to day, add a managed security offer on Huntress plus an MSP partner, and hire a security engineer. Otherwise hire a second consultant.

This is the stage where Caprius can become the MSSP you planned, built on clients who already trust you.
